# Modulus equations and inequalities

> **AHL 2.16 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Absolute value records distance from zero. An equation $|x-a|=r$ with $r\geq0$ gives points a distance $r$ from $a$; the corresponding strict inequality $|x-a|<r$ describes an open interval around $a$. Piecewise reasoning explains more complicated graphs.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$|u|=u$ if $u\geq0$, and $|u|=-u$ if $u<0$. For $r>0$, $|x-a|<r\iff a-r<x<a+r$; $|x-a|>r$ gives two exterior rays. Always check signs in each piece.

## Worked example

Solve $|x-2|=3$. The two distance equations are $x-2=3$ and $x-2=-3$, so $x=5$ or $x=-1$. For $|x-2|<3$, we need $-3<x-2<3$, giving $-1<x<5$. On a graph, $y=|x-2|$ is a V with vertex $(2,0)$.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Break $|g(x)|$ into branches at the zeroes of $g$.
2. Solve modulus equations subject to each branch condition.
3. Interpret $|g(x)|<c$ and $|g(x)|>c$ as distance regions.

## Why the method works

Absolute value folds negative outputs above the horizontal axis. This fold creates corners where the inside expression crosses zero, so a graph of $|g(x)|$ differs from the graph of $g(x)$ only on originally negative parts. An equation with a negative right-hand side has no solution; squaring both sides without that check can generate invalid candidates.

## A contrasting worked route

Solve $|2x-1|=3$: either $2x-1=3$, giving $x=2$, or $2x-1=-3$, giving $x=-1$. Both satisfy the original equation. By contrast $|2x-1|<3$ gives $-3<2x-1<3$ and $-1<x<2$. The equation has two boundary points; the inequality contains the interval between them.

## Transfer and validation

If the right side also depends on $x$, mark where it is nonnegative before separating branches. On a sketch, intersections with a horizontal line correspond to solutions, but a graph alone cannot establish exact endpoints. Substitute each branch solution back into the original absolute-value statement.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: explain the two cases or the distance interpretation; do not omit a valid branch. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

If the right side of $|u|=c$ is negative, there are no real solutions because absolute value is nonnegative.

## Try it yourself

Solve $|2x+1|=5$.

## Checked answer

$2x+1=\pm5$ gives $x=2$ or $x=-3$.
