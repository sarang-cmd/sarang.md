# Quadratic equations and inequalities

> **SL 2.7 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Solving a quadratic equation finds boundary points; solving a quadratic inequality also asks which intervals satisfy a sign condition. The sign of the leading coefficient and the roots tell you which intervals to test. Equality affects whether endpoints belong to the answer.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$x=(-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac})/(2a)$ for $ax^2+bx+c=0$, $a\neq0$. Factoring is often faster when integers work. For inequalities, use a sign chart or a clear sketch; never infer an interval from the roots alone.

## Worked example

Solve $x^2-5x+6<0$. Factor $(x-2)(x-3)<0$. The roots $2$ and $3$ split the line into three intervals. Test $x=0$: positive; test $x=5/2$: negative; test $x=4$: positive. Therefore $2<x<3$. The strict inequality excludes both roots.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Factor or complete the square to solve exact equations.
2. Use the quadratic formula and classify roots from the discriminant.
3. Build a sign chart for strict or inclusive quadratic inequalities.

## Why the method works

The equation $ax^2+bx+c=0$ isolates zeroes, but an inequality asks where the expression has a particular sign. A positive leading coefficient makes the graph rise outside its two real roots; a negative leading coefficient reverses the signs. If the discriminant is negative, there are no real crossings, so the sign is constant.

## A contrasting worked route

Solve $-x^2+4x-3\geq0$ by factoring $-(x-1)(x-3)\geq0$. The parabola is nonnegative from $1$ through $3$, so $1\leq x\leq3$. Both endpoints are allowed because equality is included. A single test value such as $x=2$ establishes the sign between the roots.

## Transfer and validation

The quadratic formula can return two numerical values, but never skip checking a model restriction such as $x>0$. For exact questions keep surds until the end. For inequality questions present an interval or union of intervals, not simply a list of roots.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: include a sign diagram or test point so the chosen interval is justified. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

For $\leq0$, include roots where the expression equals zero. For $<0$, exclude them.

## Try it yourself

Solve $x^2-4\geq0$.

## Checked answer

$(x-2)(x+2)\geq0$ for $x\leq-2$ or $x\geq2$.
