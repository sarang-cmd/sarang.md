# Reciprocal and simple rational functions

> **SL 2.8 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

A rational graph can have vertical asymptotes, horizontal asymptotes or holes. The algebraic domain must be checked before simplifying. A hole occurs when a factor cancels, whereas an uncancelled denominator zero produces a vertical blow-up in typical simple cases.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$f(x)=a+k/(x-h)$ has vertical asymptote $x=h$ if $k\neq0$ and horizontal asymptote $y=a$. To locate intercepts, solve numerator $=0$ for the $x$-intercept and substitute $x=0$ for the $y$-intercept, if allowed.

## Worked example

Rewrite $(x+1)/(x-2)$ as $1+3/(x-2)$, because $x+1=(x-2)+3$. Thus $x=2$ and $y=1$ are asymptotes. The $x$-intercept is $-1$; at $x=0$, $y=-1/2$. The graph cannot include $x=2$, and the two branches approach but do not touch the vertical line.

## Why the method works

The formula $a/(x-h)+k$ comes from stretching and shifting $1/x$. The denominator restriction creates a missing input at $x=h$, while a nonzero numerator prevents the output from equaling $k$. Cross-multiplication is valid only after noting excluded values. A simplified expression can conceal a hole inherited from its original denominator.

## A contrasting worked route

For $f(x)=5/(x+2)-1$, exclude $x=-2$, and since the numerator is nonzero, exclude $y=-1$ from the range. To solve $f(x)=4$, write $5/(x+2)=5$ to get $x=-1$, which is allowed. Multiplying both sides by $x+2$ without first checking $x=-2$ can produce an illegal candidate in a different example.

## Transfer and validation

As $x$ approaches a vertical asymptote from opposite sides the values can go toward different infinities. As $|x|$ grows, the reciprocal term tends to zero and the graph approaches its horizontal level. An asymptote describes a limit; it is not a substitute for stating the domain.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: justify the asymptotes by division and mark at least one intercept. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

An asymptote is not necessarily a line the graph never crosses. A horizontal asymptote describes behavior at large $|x|$.

## Try it yourself

State the asymptotes of $y=2+5/(x+1)$.

## Checked answer

Vertical $x=-1$; horizontal $y=2$.
