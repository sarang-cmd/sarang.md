# Rational and other inequalities

> **AHL 2.15 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

An inequality with a denominator changes sign when that denominator changes sign. Multiplying both sides by an unknown-sign expression can silently reverse the inequality, so a sign chart is safer. Endpoints where the numerator vanishes may be included; poles never are.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

Factor numerator and denominator, mark every zero and excluded point, test one input in each interval, and apply strict or inclusive endpoints carefully. When multiplying by a known negative constant, reverse the inequality direction.

## Worked example

Solve $(x-1)/(x+2)\geq0$. Critical points are $x=-2$ (excluded) and $x=1$ (zero). For $x<-2$, both factors are negative, so the quotient is positive. For $-2<x<1$, it is negative. For $x>1$, it is positive. The answer is $(-\infty,-2)\cup[1,\infty)$.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Place zeroes and denominator exclusions on a sign chart.
2. Solve strict or inclusive rational inequalities.
3. Treat an absolute-value inequality as a distance or split it into cases.

## Why the method works

The sign of a rational expression can change at numerator zeroes and denominator zeroes. A numerator zero may be included under $\leq$ or $\geq$; a denominator zero is never included. Multiplying an inequality by an unknown-sign denominator without cases can reverse its direction unexpectedly. A sign chart avoids this trap.

## A contrasting worked route

For $(x-2)/(x+1)>0$, critical values are $-1$ and $2$. Testing $x=-2,0,3$ shows positive signs on $(-\infty,-1)$ and $(2,\infty)$. Exclude $-1$ because the expression is undefined and exclude $2$ because the inequality is strict. The interval $(-1,2)$ is negative, not positive.

## Transfer and validation

A modulus inequality such as $|x-a|<r$ describes a radius-$r$ neighborhood around $a$ when $r>0$. When graphing the solution, hollow versus solid endpoints represent strict versus inclusive inequalities. Always verify one sample from each interval, especially after squaring or cross-multiplying.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: present a sign table or test values and label the pole separately from a root. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

Never include $x=-2$ just because the inequality includes equality. A division by zero is undefined.

## Try it yourself

Solve $(x-3)/(x+1)>0$.

## Checked answer

The quotient is positive on $(-\infty,-1)\cup(3,\infty)$; exclude both boundaries.
