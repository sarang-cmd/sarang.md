# Quadratic functions

> **SL 2.6 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

A quadratic graph is a parabola. Its completed-square form makes the vertex and direction visible, while factored form makes roots visible. Every quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient has exactly one axis of symmetry.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$ has vertex $(h,k)$ and opens upward if $a>0$, downward if $a<0$. The discriminant $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ indicates two, one or no distinct real roots as $\Delta$ is positive, zero or negative.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 3 (PDF p. 5), lists axis of symmetry of a quadratic under SL 2.6. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

For $f(x)=-2(x-3)^2+8$, the vertex is $(3,8)$ and the graph opens downward. Solve $-2(x-3)^2+8=0$: $(x-3)^2=4$, so $x=1$ or $x=5$. The roots are equally spaced from the axis $x=3$. The maximum value of $f$ is $8$.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Convert among expanded, factorized and completed-square forms.
2. Locate the vertex and axis of symmetry.
3. Use the discriminant to predict the number of real roots.

## Why the method works

Each algebraic form answers a different graph question efficiently. The factorized form exposes roots; the squared form exposes the vertex; the expanded form exposes the vertical intercept. Completing the square amounts to translating a basic parabola without altering its shape. The sign of the leading coefficient determines whether the vertex is a minimum or maximum.

## A contrasting worked route

For $f(x)=-2(x-1)^2+8$, the vertex is $(1,8)$ and the axis is $x=1$. Setting $f=0$ gives $(x-1)^2=4$, so $x=-1$ or $3$. Expanding gives $-2x^2+4x+6$, whose $y$-intercept is $6$. All three descriptions refer to the same curve.

## Transfer and validation

When a quadratic models length or time, its mathematical roots may be outside the contextual domain. Give units for coordinates representing measurable quantities. Use symmetry to check two computed roots: their midpoint should match the axis, even when roots are irrational or found numerically.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: move between completed-square and factored representations to explain roots and extremum. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

In $a(x-h)^2+k$, the vertex has $x=h$, not $-h$. Check by making the squared term zero.

## Try it yourself

Give the vertex of $y=3(x+2)^2-5$.

## Checked answer

$(-2,-5)$, and this is a minimum because $3>0$.
