# Exponential and logarithmic functions

> **SL 2.9 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Exponential growth changes by a constant ratio over equal input steps. A logarithmic function reverses a positive-base exponential. Their graphs are reflections across $y=x$ when paired as inverses, with domain and range exchanged.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

For $b>0$, $b\neq1$, $f(x)=b^x$ has range $(0,\infty)$; $f^{-1}(x)=\log_b x$ has domain $(0,\infty)$. When $b>1$, both functions increase. Growth models of the form $A b^t$ require units and interpretation of $t$.

## Worked example

For $f(x)=3^x$, solve $f(x)=27$ by writing $27=3^3$: $x=3$. The inverse is $f^{-1}(x)=\log_3 x$ for $x>0$. Check the inverse at $x=27$: $\log_3 27=3$. The exponential passes through $(0,1)$; the log passes through $(1,0)$.

## Why the method works

For a positive base other than one, exponential outputs remain positive, so the inverse log needs positive inputs. Increasing bases above one grow; bases strictly between zero and one decay. Adding a constant outside the exponential changes the horizontal asymptote, while shifting an input inside the log changes its vertical asymptote and domain.

## A contrasting worked route

For $f(x)=2^x-3$, the range is $(-3,\infty)$ and the horizontal asymptote is $y=-3$. The zero solves $2^x=3$, hence $x=\log_2 3$. Its inverse satisfies $y=\log_2(x+3)$ on $x>-3$. The shifted log domain is determined by $x+3>0$.

## Transfer and validation

An exponential model assumes a constant multiplicative change per equal time step; a log transformation can linearize that idealized behavior. Check whether data really support that assumption. Use a calculator for an approximate inverse only after writing an exact log expression and its domain.

## Exam lens

Paper 2 style: use a graph or numerical solve when exponentials cannot be reduced to a common base; interpret the answer in context. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

A logarithmic graph does not include $x=0$. Its vertical asymptote reflects the exponential horizontal asymptote $y=0$.

## Try it yourself

State the domain of $y=\ln(x-4)$ and solve $e^x=e^2$.

## Checked answer

Domain $x>4$ and solution $x=2$.
