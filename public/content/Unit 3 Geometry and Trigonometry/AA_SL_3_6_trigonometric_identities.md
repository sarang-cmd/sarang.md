# Trigonometric identities

> **SL 3.6 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

An identity holds for all inputs in its common domain, rather than only for the solutions of one equation. The Pythagorean identity follows from the unit circle. Equivalent forms can replace a sine square with a cosine square or simplify a mixed expression.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ and $\tan x=\sin x/\cos x$ when $\cos x\neq0$. Dividing the first identity by $\cos^2x$ gives $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$ where defined.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 7), lists Pythagorean and double-angle identities under SL 3.6. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

Suppose $\cos x=3/5$ and $x$ is in quadrant IV. Then $\sin^2x=1-9/25=16/25$. Quadrant IV has negative sine, so $\sin x=-4/5$. Consequently $\tan x=(-4/5)/(3/5)=-4/3$. Substitution into $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ checks the result.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Derive or apply $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$.
2. Switch among three equivalent forms of $\cos2x$.
3. Prove an identity on a domain where every division is defined.

## Why the method works

The Pythagorean identity is the coordinate equation of the unit circle. Double-angle identities follow from compound-angle formulas and can be rewritten using the same Pythagorean relation. Choose the form that leaves one trigonometric function to solve for; do not divide by a function that can be zero without excluding those values.

## A contrasting worked route

If $\cos x=3/5$ and $x$ is in quadrant IV, $\sin x=-4/5$. Hence $\sin2x=2(3/5)(-4/5)=-24/25$ and $\cos2x=2(3/5)^2-1=-7/25$. The interval information determined the sine sign; square roots alone would not.

## Transfer and validation

To prove an identity, work from one side toward the other while stating any denominator restrictions. Verifying it at a few angles is a check but not a proof. If an equation is created from an identity, substitute final roots in the original equation, especially after squaring.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: state sign information as well as the identity; a square root by itself has two signs. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

From $\sin^2x=16/25$ it does not follow that $\sin x=4/5$ without a quadrant condition.

## Try it yourself

If $\sin x=5/13$ and $x$ is in quadrant II, find $\cos x$.

## Checked answer

$\cos^2x=1-25/169=144/169$, and quadrant II gives $\cos x=-12/13$.
