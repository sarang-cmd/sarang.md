# Relationships among trigonometric functions

> **AHL 3.11 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Double-angle and related identities make it possible to move between known values at $x$ and expressions at $2x$. The quadrant of the original angle determines any missing signs. Identity manipulation is also useful for converting an equation into one trigonometric function.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x$, $\cos2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x=1-2\sin^2x=2\cos^2x-1$. These are equal forms of the same identity, not separate restrictions.

## Worked example

Suppose $\sin x=4/5$ and $\cos x=3/5$ for an acute $x$. Then $\sin2x=2(4/5)(3/5)=24/25$ and $\cos2x=(3/5)^2-(4/5)^2=-7/25$. The negative cosine is reasonable because $2x$ lies in quadrant II for this triangle.

## Why the method works

A trigonometric ratio is not an isolated formula: tangent is a quotient and the reciprocal ratios are defined by the other two coordinates. Algebraic substitutions among these relationships are valid only where their denominators exist. Squaring both sides loses sign information, which can create apparent solutions outside a specified quadrant.

## A contrasting worked route

If $\sin x=-3/5$ and $x$ is in quadrant IV, then $\cos x=4/5$, $\tan x=-3/4$ and $\sec x=5/4$. The Pythagorean identity yields $|\cos x|=4/5$ first; the quadrant selects the positive sign. Every inferred ratio should be checked against its denominator condition.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: choose the double-angle form with the fewest unknown quantities. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

The sign of $\cos2x$ is not necessarily the sign of $\cos x$. Doubling may move the terminal side to another quadrant.

## Try it yourself

If $\sin x=1/2$ and $x$ is acute, find $\cos2x$.

## Checked answer

$1-2(1/2)^2=1/2$.
