# Vector concepts in two and three dimensions

> **AHL 3.12 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

A vector has direction and magnitude. Subtracting position vectors gives displacement from one point to another. A unit vector has magnitude one and records direction without distance. Distinguish a point's coordinates from the displacement between points.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

For $\mathbf v=(v_1,v_2,v_3)$, $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{v_1^2+v_2^2+v_3^2}$ and its unit direction is $\mathbf v/|\mathbf v|$ when $\mathbf v\neq\mathbf0$. $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 6 (PDF p. 8), lists magnitude of a three-dimensional vector under AHL 3.12. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

Let $A=(1,2,3)$ and $B=(4,6,3)$. Then $\overrightarrow{AB}=(4-1,6-2,3-3)=(3,4,0)$ and its magnitude is $\sqrt{9+16}=5$. The unit vector from $A$ toward $B$ is $(3/5,4/5,0)$. Its squared magnitude is $9/25+16/25=1$.

1. Use directed components to add, subtract and scale vectors.

## Why the method works

A vector specifies magnitude and direction rather than a location by itself. Subtracting position vectors gives displacement from the first point to the second; reversing the order reverses its direction but keeps its magnitude. The norm comes from Pythagoras in perpendicular coordinate directions. A unit vector exists only when the original vector is nonzero.

## A contrasting worked route

From $P=(1,-2,3)$ to $Q=(5,1,3)$, displacement is $\overrightarrow{PQ}=(4,3,0)$, of length $5$ and unit direction $(4/5,3/5,0)$. Reversing the arrow gives $(-4,-3,0)$, also length $5$. Adding coordinates of the endpoints would not represent the requested displacement.

## Transfer and validation

A scalar multiple changes length and possibly reverses direction, while vector addition represents successive displacements. State a coordinate system and unit when the vector represents physical movement. Distinguish a point such as $(4,3)$ from a displacement with the same ordered components.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: write the subtraction in the correct order; a diagram helps distinguish $AB$ from $BA$. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

$\overrightarrow{BA}$ is the negative of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, not the same vector, even though their lengths are equal.

## Try it yourself

Find the vector from $(2,-1)$ to $(5,3)$.

## Checked answer

$(3,4)$, of magnitude $5$.
