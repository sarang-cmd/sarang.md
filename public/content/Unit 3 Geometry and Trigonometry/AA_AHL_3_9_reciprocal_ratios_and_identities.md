# Reciprocal ratios and identities

> **AHL 3.9 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Reciprocal trigonometric ratios are convenient shorthand, but each has excluded inputs where its denominator is zero. Their Pythagorean identities follow by dividing $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ by a nonzero sine or cosine square.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sec x=1/\cos x$, $\csc x=1/\sin x$, and $\cot x=\cos x/\sin x$. Where defined, $1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x$ and $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$. Do not cancel across values at which the original functions are undefined.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 7), lists reciprocal identities and Pythagorean relations under AHL 3.9. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

If $\sin x=3/5$ and $\cos x=4/5$, then $\csc x=5/3$ and $\cot x=(4/5)/(3/5)=4/3$. Check $1+(4/3)^2=25/9=(5/3)^2$. The equation is valid because $\sin x\neq0$.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Define secant, cosecant and cotangent with excluded denominator zeroes.
2. Use $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$ or $1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x$.
3. Choose signs of reciprocal functions from quadrant information.

## Why the method works

A reciprocal ratio inherits every zero exclusion of its denominator. The identities arise by dividing $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ by $\cos^2x$ or $\sin^2x$ on domains where those quantities are nonzero. The squares determine magnitude but not sign; quadrant information must settle the sign of a reciprocal value.

## A contrasting worked route

If $\tan x=3/4$ in quadrant III, construct a reference triangle with hypotenuse $5$. Both sine and cosine are negative, so $\sec x=-5/4$ and $\csc x=-5/3$. The identity $1+\tan^2x=25/16=\sec^2x$ confirms the magnitude but cannot determine the negative sign on its own.

## Transfer and validation

The reciprocal of tangent is cotangent, not the inverse function $\arctan$. Writing definitions before manipulation prevents this notation confusion. When solving identities containing ratios, state which angles were excluded by division even if a rearranged equation appears defined there. A real secant or cosecant has magnitude at least one wherever defined, providing another useful check.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: derive an identity from the unit-circle equation instead of treating reciprocal notation as a new operation. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

$\sin^{-1}x$ usually denotes arcsine, not $1/\sin x$. Use $\csc x$ for the reciprocal.

## Try it yourself

If $\cos x=1/2$, what is $\sec x$?

## Checked answer

$\sec x=2$.
