# Scalar product and angles

> **AHL 3.13 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

The scalar or dot product measures directional alignment. A zero dot product for nonzero vectors signals perpendicularity; a negative result signals an obtuse angle between them. It is a number, not a vector.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$ for nonzero vectors. Thus $\cos\theta=(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)/(|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|)$.

## Worked example

Take $\mathbf a=(1,2,2)$ and $\mathbf b=(2,0,-1)$. Their dot product is $1(2)+2(0)+2(-1)=0$. Both are nonzero, so $\cos\theta=0$ and the vectors are perpendicular. Their magnitudes are $3$ and $\sqrt5$, which are nonzero as required.

## Why the method works

The dot product records how much one vector points along another. Component multiplication adds contributions from each axis; the geometric form divides by both magnitudes to recover an angle. A zero dot product implies perpendicularity for nonzero vectors, not that either vector must be zero. The cosine must lie between $-1$ and $1$.

## A contrasting worked route

For $\mathbf u=(2,1,0)$ and $\mathbf v=(1,-2,2)$, the dot product is $2-2+0=0$, so they meet at $90^\circ$. Their magnitudes are $\sqrt5$ and $3$. These values confirm that neither is zero; without that condition an angle between vectors would be undefined.

## Transfer and validation

A negative dot product gives an obtuse angle, but not the exact angle without the magnitudes. In an applied projection, keep track of whether you seek a scalar component or a vector. Use an independent numerical check of your cosine before evaluating an inverse cosine on a calculator.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: show component multiplication and check the vectors are nonzero before concluding perpendicularity. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

A zero vector has zero dot product with everything but has no well-defined direction or angle.

## Try it yourself

Find $(3,-1)\cdot(2,4)$.

## Checked answer

$3(2)+(-1)(4)=2$.
