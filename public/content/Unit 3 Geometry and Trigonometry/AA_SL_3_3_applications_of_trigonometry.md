# Applications of trigonometry

> **SL 3.3 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Trigonometry connects diagrams with real measurements: bearings, heights, inaccessible distances and inclined surfaces. Bearings are measured clockwise from north, unlike standard coordinate angles measured counterclockwise from the positive horizontal direction.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

For non-right triangles use $a/\sin A=b/\sin B=c/\sin C$ when opposite side-angle pairs are known, or $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$ when two sides and the included angle are known. Check the ambiguous sine-rule case where two triangles may fit.

## Worked example

A walker travels $6$ km east and $8$ km north. The displacement is $\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10$ km. From north, the direction turns toward east by $\arctan(6/8)\approx36.87^\circ$, so the bearing is approximately $037^\circ$. A sketch prevents measuring the angle from east instead.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Model heights and horizontal distances from elevation or depression angles.

3. Combine two sight lines using triangle rules and realistic units.

## Why the method works

An angle of elevation is measured above a horizontal line at the observer, not from a vertical mast. Bearings are measured clockwise from north and require a direction reference at each location. A sketch with horizontal and vertical axes makes a right triangle visible before any trigonometric calculation.

## A contrasting worked route

A tower subtends an elevation angle of $45^\circ$ from a point $20$ metres from its base on level ground. Its height is $20\tan45^\circ=20$ metres. If the observer stands on a $2$ metre platform, the tower top is $22$ metres above ground, not $20$; the trig calculation found only the difference in heights.

## Transfer and validation

A practical answer depends on the modeled ground being level and the observation points being correct. When two sightings are provided, keep a single diagram with the same north arrow or baseline. Round a final distance to the requested precision and check that it is compatible with the geometry.

## Exam lens

Paper 2 style: name the north reference, show the triangle, and round a bearing to three digits if appropriate. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

$37^\circ$ as a coordinate angle from east is a different direction from a bearing of $037^\circ$ from north.

## Try it yourself

A point lies $3$ km east and $4$ km north of the start. Find its straight-line distance.

## Checked answer

$\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$ km.
