# Compound-angle identities

> **AHL 3.10 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Compound-angle formulas express the trigonometric value of a sum or difference in terms of the separate angles. They give exact values of less familiar angles and underpin double-angle identities. Signs must follow the particular formula.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B\pm\cos A\sin B$ and $\cos(A\pm B)=\cos A\cos B\mp\sin A\sin B$. Set $B=A$ to get $\sin2A=2\sin A\cos A$.

## Worked example

To find $\sin75^\circ$, write $75^\circ=45^\circ+30^\circ$. Then $\sin75^\circ=\sin45^\circ\cos30^\circ+\cos45^\circ\sin30^\circ=(\sqrt2/2)(\sqrt3/2)+(\sqrt2/2)(1/2)=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/4$. This is positive and close to one, as the angle suggests.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Expand sine and cosine of a sum or difference.
2. Apply the tangent addition formula only when its denominator is nonzero.
3. Derive exact double-angle values from known angles.

## Why the method works

Compound-angle identities combine two rotations, and the sign in the cosine formula reverses the sign in the angle. It is often safer to write the entire identity with $A$ and $B$ before inserting numerical angles. For tangent, $1-\tan A\tan B=0$ signals an undefined tangent of the sum, not an infinite numeric solution.

## A contrasting worked route

Using $\sin(45^\circ+30^\circ)$ gives $(\sqrt2/2)(\sqrt3/2)+(\sqrt2/2)(1/2)=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/4$. The corresponding cosine is $(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/4$ because its cross term is subtracted. Approximate $\sin75^\circ$ to confirm it lies near one, not near zero.

## Transfer and validation

When the problem mixes degrees and radians, convert before combining values. The double-angle formulas are special cases with equal angles; decide whether to express the answer in sine, cosine or tangent to match available data. State a restriction whenever a ratio includes a vanishing denominator. Recheck signs by substituting one angle pair into the unexpanded trigonometric expression.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: select special angles whose sum gives the target and keep radicals exact. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

Cosine of a sum has a minus sign in the middle; copying the sine sign gives a wrong identity.

## Try it yourself

Find $\cos75^\circ$ in exact form.

## Checked answer

$\cos(45^\circ+30^\circ)=(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/4$.
