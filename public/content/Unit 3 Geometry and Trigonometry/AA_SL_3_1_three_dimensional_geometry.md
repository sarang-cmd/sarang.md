# Three-dimensional geometry

> **SL 3.1 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Three-dimensional distances come from repeated right triangles. A diagram helps identify which length is horizontal, vertical or oblique. A true spatial diagonal is not the same as a face diagonal, and a context may restrict which route can actually be traveled.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

For a rectangular box with perpendicular side lengths $a,b,c$, the spatial diagonal is $d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$. Derive it by finding the base diagonal $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and then applying Pythagoras with the height $c$.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 4 (PDF p. 6), lists three-dimensional distance, midpoint and pyramid volume under SL 3.1. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

A cuboid measures $3$, $4$ and $12$ units along three mutually perpendicular edges. Its base diagonal is $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$. The spatial diagonal is $\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13$. Direct calculation gives $\sqrt{9+16+144}=13$, confirming the two-stage construction.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Find three-dimensional lengths and midpoints with coordinates.
2. Use right-triangle sections of solids to locate hidden distances.
3. Choose volume or surface-area formulas that match the shape and dimensions.

## Why the method works

A three-dimensional distance combines three perpendicular coordinate differences by applying Pythagoras twice. Drawing an appropriate cross-section turns an unfamiliar solid into a familiar right triangle. For volume, distinguish height perpendicular to the base from a sloping edge; replacing one by the other gives a systematically wrong answer.

## A contrasting worked route

Points $(1,2,3)$ and $(4,6,15)$ are $\sqrt{3^2+4^2+12^2}=13$ units apart. Their midpoint is $(5/2,4,9)$; check that the coordinate changes from the midpoint to either endpoint are opposites. For a right pyramid with base area $24$ and height $6$, volume is $24(6)/3=48$, not $144$.

## Transfer and validation

Attach units to lengths, square units to areas and cubic units to volumes. If a problem supplies a diagram, label the actual perpendicular height before using a formula booklet entry. Coordinate answers should remain exact when possible; round only when a physical measurement requires it.

## Exam lens

Paper 2 style: draw the right triangle used at each stage, state units, and check whether the requested distance is inside or on the surface. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

Adding edge lengths gives a path along edges, not the straight-line diagonal. Use perpendicular squares for straight-line distance.

## Try it yourself

Find the spatial diagonal of a $2\times3\times6$ cuboid.

## Checked answer

$\sqrt{2^2+3^2+6^2}=\sqrt{49}=7$ units.
