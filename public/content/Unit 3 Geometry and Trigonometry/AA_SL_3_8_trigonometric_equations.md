# Trigonometric equations

> **SL 3.8 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

A trigonometric equation usually has more than one solution because functions repeat. Find a reference angle, select the quadrants with the correct sign, then include all periods that lie in the requested interval. An inverse-trig button returns only a principal value.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sin x=\sin\alpha$ gives $x=\alpha+2k\pi$ or $x=\pi-\alpha+2k\pi$ for integer $k$. For cosine and tangent use their corresponding symmetries and periods. Always state whether the interval endpoints are included.

## Worked example

Solve $2\sin x=1$ for $0\leq x<2\pi$. Then $\sin x=1/2$. The reference angle is $\pi/6$ and sine is positive in quadrants I and II. Thus $x=\pi/6$ or $x=5\pi/6$. Neither excluded endpoint $2\pi$ is a solution.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Solve sine, cosine or tangent equations over an exact interval.
2. Transform an angle such as $2x$ before enumerating candidates.
3. Reject solutions introduced by division or inverse-function shortcuts.

## Why the method works

Inverse trigonometric functions return principal values. Periodicity and symmetry supply other solutions: sine repeats every $2\pi$ and has reflection symmetry, while tangent repeats every $\pi$. If the unknown appears inside $2x$, double the bounds of the requested interval before solving, then halve the candidates.

## A contrasting worked route

For $\sin x=1/2$ on $0\leq x<2\pi$, the solutions are $\pi/6$ and $5\pi/6$. For $\sin(2x)=1/2$ on $0\leq x<\pi$, the angle $2x$ has the same full-turn interval, so $x=\pi/12$ and $5\pi/12$. Failing to change the interval causes missing or extra solutions.

## Transfer and validation

Mark whether endpoints are included. A calculator in degrees when the question uses radians can give plausible-looking but incompatible decimals. Verify each exact root with a known unit-circle value and state all solutions requested, not an unqualified general solution.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: show the reference angle and both valid quadrants; Paper 2 can check the interval graphically. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

Returning only $\arcsin(1/2)=\pi/6$ misses the quadrant II solution.

## Try it yourself

Solve $\cos x=0$ for $0\leq x<2\pi$.

## Checked answer

$x=\pi/2$ or $x=3\pi/2$.
