# Trigonometric ratios

> **SL 3.2 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

In a right triangle, sine, cosine and tangent relate an angle to particular side ratios. Label opposite and adjacent relative to the angle you are using, not relative to how the page is rotated. Exact special-angle ratios are useful checks on calculator answers.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\sin\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{hypotenuse}$, $\cos\theta=\text{adjacent}/\text{hypotenuse}$ and $\tan\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{adjacent}$ for an acute angle in a right triangle. Check the calculator's degree or radian mode.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 7), lists sine rule, cosine rule and triangle area under SL 3.2. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

A right triangle has hypotenuse $10$ and an acute angle of $30^\circ$. The opposite side is $10\sin30^\circ=10(1/2)=5$. By Pythagoras the adjacent side is $\sqrt{10^2-5^2}=5\sqrt3$, agreeing with $10\cos30^\circ$. The ratios depend on the chosen angle.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Use right-triangle sine, cosine and tangent with a consistent angle convention.
2. Select sine or cosine rule for non-right triangles.
3. Find triangle area from two sides and the included angle.

## Why the method works

The sine rule connects each side to its opposite angle, while the cosine rule handles an included angle and two sides or all three sides. These are not interchangeable data patterns. When solving an angle with inverse sine, another angle may have the same sine; triangle angle sum and side ordering decide whether an ambiguous second triangle is possible.

## A contrasting worked route

With sides $a=5$, $b=7$ and included angle $C=60^\circ$, the cosine rule gives $c^2=25+49-70(1/2)=39$, so $c=\sqrt{39}$. The same data give area $\tfrac12(5)(7)\sin60^\circ=35\sqrt3/4$. Using $60^\circ$ opposite side $5$ in the sine rule would mislabel the diagram.

## Transfer and validation

Set the calculator to the units used by the question, degrees or radians. Draw a rough triangle and check that the longest side faces the largest angle. For a right triangle the general rules still hold, but a direct right-triangle ratio is usually simpler.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: show which ratio matches the known and unknown sides; Paper 2 can estimate non-special angles. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

A result longer than the hypotenuse for a side of the same right triangle is a warning that the sides or calculator mode are wrong.

## Try it yourself

If the legs are $6$ and $8$, find the hypotenuse and the sine of the angle opposite the side $6$.

## Checked answer

Hypotenuse $10$ and sine $6/10=3/5$.
