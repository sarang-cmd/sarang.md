# The unit circle

> **SL 3.5 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

On a circle of radius one, the point at angle $\theta$ has coordinates $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. Quadrant signs and symmetry allow exact values for related angles. This extends trigonometry beyond acute right-triangle angles.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$ follows from the circle equation $x^2+y^2=1$. Adding $2\pi$ leaves the same point. In quadrant II, sine is positive and cosine is negative.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 7), lists tangent ratio on the unit circle under SL 3.5. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

For $\theta=5\pi/6$, the reference angle is $\pi/6$ and the terminal point lies in quadrant II. Therefore $\sin(5\pi/6)=1/2$ and $\cos(5\pi/6)=-\sqrt3/2$. Their quotient gives $\tan(5\pi/6)=-1/\sqrt3$. Check $1/4+3/4=1$.

## Why the method works

On the unit circle the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. Angles differing by a full revolution reach the same point. The reference angle supplies magnitude; the quadrant supplies signs. Tangent is a ratio of these coordinates, so it is undefined where the cosine coordinate vanishes.

## A contrasting worked route

At $5\pi/6$, the reference angle is $\pi/6$ in quadrant II. Thus $\cos(5\pi/6)=-\sqrt3/2$, $\sin(5\pi/6)=1/2$, and $\tan(5\pi/6)=-1/\sqrt3$. This is a sign check, not a calculator approximation. At $\pi/2$, tangent has no finite real value.

## Transfer and validation

Use exact values to verify a calculator set to radians. When solving a trigonometric equation, an inverse function often supplies only a principal angle, not every angle in the required interval. Sketch the unit circle and add complete periods to enumerate the other candidates.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: mark the quadrant and reference angle before writing exact trigonometric values. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

The reference angle alone does not determine signs. The actual quadrant does.

## Try it yourself

Find $\sin(7\pi/6)$ and $\cos(7\pi/6)$.

## Checked answer

Quadrant III with reference angle $\pi/6$: $-1/2$ and $-\sqrt3/2$.
