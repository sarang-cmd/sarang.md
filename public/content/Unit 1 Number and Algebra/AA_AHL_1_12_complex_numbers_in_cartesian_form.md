# Complex numbers in Cartesian form

> **AHL 1.12 · AA HL extension** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Complex numbers extend the real line by allowing $i^2=-1$. Cartesian form records a real and imaginary coordinate, so the Argand diagram represents addition geometrically as vector addition. Conjugation reflects a point in the real axis.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

$z=a+bi$, $\overline z=a-bi$, $|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, and $z\overline z=|z|^2$. For $w\neq0$, multiply $z/w$ by $\overline w/\overline w$ to obtain a real denominator.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 3 (PDF p. 5), lists Cartesian representation of complex numbers under AHL 1.12. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

Let $z=2+3i$ and $w=1-2i$. Multiply: $zw=2-4i+3i-6i^2=8-i$. Plot $z$ at $(2,3)$ on the Argand plane. Its modulus is $\sqrt{2^2+3^2}=\sqrt{13}$ and its conjugate is $2-3i$. The modulus is the distance from the origin.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Add and multiply in Cartesian form using $i^2=-1$.
2. Divide by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate.
3. Find modulus and describe the location of a complex number in the Argand plane.

## Why the method works

Complex arithmetic behaves like polynomial arithmetic in $i$ except that every $i^2$ is replaced by $-1$. Multiplying by a conjugate produces the positive real denominator $a^2+b^2$ whenever $a+bi\neq0$. The modulus is a distance from the origin, not the sum of absolute values of the coordinates.

## A contrasting worked route

For $z=2-3i$, the conjugate is $2+3i$ and $z\overline z=4+9=13$, hence $|z|=\sqrt{13}$. Dividing $1$ by $z$ gives $(2+3i)/13$. Check by multiplying back: $(2-3i)(2+3i)/13=1$. The point $(2,-3)$ lies in the fourth quadrant, which matters when later choosing an argument.

## Transfer and validation

Equating two complex numbers means equating real parts and imaginary parts separately. A statement about modulus alone cannot determine the original number because many points lie on the same circle. Move to polar form when multiplication, division or powers would otherwise require repeated Cartesian expansion.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: draw or describe the Argand position and simplify every occurrence of $i^2$. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

The modulus of $a+bi$ is not $a+b$. It is a nonnegative distance found by Pythagoras.

## Try it yourself

Simplify $(3+i)(2-i)$ and find $|3+i|$.

## Checked answer

$7-i$ and $\sqrt{10}$.
