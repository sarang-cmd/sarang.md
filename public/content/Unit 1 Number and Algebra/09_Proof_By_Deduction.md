# Proof by Deduction

A **deductive proof** begins with facts you are allowed to use and follows them to a conclusion that must hold. Unlike checking a few examples, it works for every case covered by the assumptions.

## Generalise with variables

Prove that the sum of two odd integers is even. Write the odd integers as \(2a+1\) and \(2b+1\), where \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\). Then

$$(2a+1)+(2b+1)=2(a+b+1).$$

Because \(a+b+1\) is an integer, the sum is twice an integer and is therefore even. The final sentence connects the algebra back to the claim.

## A proof has a direction

1. State what your variables represent.
2. Start from the given conditions, not from the desired result.
3. Transform using valid steps.
4. Name the definition that makes the conclusion true.

**Try it:** Prove that the product of two odd integers is odd. Use \(2a+1\) and \(2b+1\), then rewrite the product in the form \(2k+1\).

> **Exam lens:** Never claim a statement is proved because it works for \(n=1,2,3\). Examples can disprove a claim, but they cannot establish a universal one.
