# Arithmetic Sequences & Series

An **arithmetic sequence** changes by the same amount each time. That amount is the common difference \(d\). For example, \(7,11,15,19,\ldots\) has \(d=4\).

## The term and sum formulas

If the first term is \(u_1\), the \(n\)th term is

$$u_n=u_1+(n-1)d.$$

Pairing the first and last terms gives the sum of the first \(n\) terms:

$$S_n=\frac{n}{2}(u_1+u_n)=\frac{n}{2}\bigl(2u_1+(n-1)d\bigr).$$

The \(n-1\) matters: there are nineteen **steps** from the first term to the twentieth.

## Worked example

For \(7,11,15,\ldots\), find the twentieth term and the sum of the first twenty terms.

- \(u_{20}=7+19(4)=83\).
- \(S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}(7+83)=900\).

You can check the sum roughly: the average of the first and last terms is \(45\), and there are twenty terms, so \(20\times45=900\).

**Try it:** An arithmetic sequence begins \(12,9,6,\ldots\). Find \(u_{15}\) and \(S_{15}\). Be careful with the sign of \(d\).

> **Exam lens:** Say whether you are finding a *term* or a *sum* before selecting a formula.
