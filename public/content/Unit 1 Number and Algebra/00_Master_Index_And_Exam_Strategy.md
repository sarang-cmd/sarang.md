# Master Index & Exam Strategy

Welcome to **Number & Algebra**. This reading path moves from patterns to proof, then from counting to the binomial theorem. The goal is not to memorise isolated tricks: it is to recognise the idea behind each question.

## Your route through the unit

| Chapter | What you will learn to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Foundations | What is the question really asking for? |
| Sequences & Series | How does the pattern continue, and what is its total? |
| Proof | Why must the statement be true for every case? |
| Counting | Are we choosing, arranging, or building in stages? |
| Binomial Theorem | Which term or coefficient do we actually need? |
| Synthesis | Which ideas connect in a single question? |

Move in order if these ideas are new, or use the outline to jump straight to a weak spot. Each note gives you a core fact, a worked example, and a short practice prompt.

## An exam-ready routine

1. **Read twice.** Underline what is given and what must be shown.
2. **Name the structure.** Is it arithmetic, geometric, a proof, or a counting problem?
3. **Set out the method.** State a formula or assumption before substituting values.
4. **Check the result.** Does the sign, size, and context make sense?

> **Exam lens:** A correct answer with no reasoning may leave marks behind. Show enough steps that someone else could follow your argument.

**Try it:** Without calculating, decide whether finding \(S_n\), proving a claim for all \(n\), and finding \(\binom{n}{r}\) call for the same type of reasoning. What is different about each task?
