# Synthesis & Exam Practice

The strongest exam questions ask you to **choose** the right idea before doing any algebra. Use this page as a short mixed review: identify the structure, write a setup, and then calculate or prove.

## Three quick problems

1. The arithmetic sequence \(5,9,13,\ldots\) has twenty terms. Find the twentieth term and the sum of those twenty terms.
2. Prove by induction that \(5^n-1\) is divisible by \(4\) for every positive integer \(n\).
3. Eight students are available for a three-person team. How many teams can be formed? Then find the coefficient of \(x^2\) in \((1+x)^8\).

## Check your approach

| Problem | Key idea | Result or step to aim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Arithmetic term, then arithmetic sum | \(u_{20}=81\), \(S_{20}=860\) |
| 2 | Base, hypothesis, inductive step | \(5^{k+1}-1=5(5^k-1)+4\) |
| 3 | Choose without order; use a binomial coefficient | \(\binom{8}{3}=56\); coefficient \(\binom{8}{2}=28\) |

For problem 2, once \(5^k-1\) is a multiple of \(4\), the displayed identity makes the next case a multiple of \(4\) as well. Don't forget to check \(n=1\) and state the conclusion.

## Before you close the book

- Can you explain **why** a method applies, not just use its formula?
- Can you tell a term \(u_n\) from a sum \(S_n\)?
- Can you distinguish a selection from an arrangement?

> **Exam lens:** Save a minute to test the size and meaning of each answer. Confidence is built one clear step at a time.
