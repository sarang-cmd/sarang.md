# Tangents and normals

> **SL 5.4 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

A tangent follows the local slope of a curve. A normal passes through the same point and is perpendicular to the tangent. Finding either line requires both its gradient and a point on the curve.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

At $x=a$, tangent gradient is $f'(a)$ and tangent equation is $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$. If $f'(a)\neq0$, normal gradient is $-1/f'(a)$. A horizontal tangent has a vertical normal $x=a$.

## Worked example

For $y=x^2$ at $x=2$, the point is $(2,4)$ and derivative $2x$ gives tangent gradient $4$. Thus $y-4=4(x-2)$, or $y=4x-4$. The normal has gradient $-1/4$ and equation $y-4=-\tfrac14(x-2)$. Both lines pass through $(2,4)$.

## Smaller skills within this topic

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Locate the point on the curve before forming a tangent.
2. Find a normal with negative reciprocal gradient when appropriate.
3. Handle horizontal tangents and vertical normals as separate cases.

## Why the method works

A tangent line matches the curve’s first-order direction at the contact point. The normal is perpendicular to that tangent, so its gradient is $-1/m$ only when the tangent gradient $m$ is nonzero and finite. A horizontal tangent has a vertical normal, which cannot be expressed as $y=mx+c$.

## A contrasting worked route

For $y=x^2$ at $x=-2$, the point is $(-2,4)$ and tangent gradient is $2x=-4$. Hence the tangent is $y-4=-4(x+2)$; the normal is $y-4=\tfrac14(x+2)$. Substituting $(-2,4)$ checks both lines, and their gradients multiply to $-1$.

## Transfer and validation

Do not substitute the $x$-coordinate into the derivative and mistake the resulting gradient for the $y$-coordinate. In a context, the tangent approximates nearby output values, whereas the normal is a geometric construction. State the contact point explicitly in the final equations.

## Exam lens

Paper 1 style: write the point before finding either line, and check the perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

The normal gradient is the negative reciprocal, not just the negative tangent gradient.

## Try it yourself

Find the tangent to $y=3x$ at $x=1$.

## Checked answer

The curve is already a line, so the tangent is $y=3x$.
