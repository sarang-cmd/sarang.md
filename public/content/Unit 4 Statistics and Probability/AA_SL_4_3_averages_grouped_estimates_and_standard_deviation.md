# Averages, grouped estimates and standard deviation

> **SL 4.3 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Measures of center and spread answer different questions. The mean uses every numerical value and is sensitive to outliers; the median locates the middle rank. A grouped-data mean uses class midpoints and is an estimate because exact values within each class are unknown.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

Population mean $\mu=\sum x_i/N$ and population standard deviation $\sigma=\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\mu)^2/N}$. A sample standard deviation convention instead uses $N-1$ in the denominator; state or select the intended measure.

**Booklet cross-check:** The supplied IBO 2023, Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL formula booklet, Version 1.0, printed p. 7 (PDF p. 9), lists weighted mean under SL 4.3. Derive and justify all other steps and conditions.

## Worked example

For $2,4,4,6$, the mean is $(2+4+4+6)/4=4$ and the median is also $4$. Squared deviations from the mean are $4,0,0,4$, summing to $8$. The population variance is $8/4=2$ and population standard deviation is $\sqrt2$. Treating the same data as a sample would give $s=\sqrt{8/3}$ instead.

Practise each skill separately, then combine them in problems:

1. Compute mean, median and mode for raw observations.

3. Compare population standard deviation and outlier sensitivity.

## Why the method works

The mean balances the total data, so every observation affects it; the median depends mainly on ordering. For grouped values, midpoint weighting is an approximation because the true positions inside classes are unknown. Standard deviation measures typical squared deviation around the mean before taking the square root; adding a distant value often changes it substantially.

## A contrasting worked route

For $2,3,3,4$, the mean is $3$, median $3$, and mode $3$. Replacing $4$ with $20$ changes the mean to $7$ but leaves the median at $3$. The first list has population variance $[(2-3)^2+0+0+(4-3)^2]/4=1/2$, illustrating how spread differs from center.

## Transfer and validation

A sample standard deviation on a calculator may use a denominator $n-1$, while population standard deviation uses $n$. Specify which statistic the question requests. Do not round class midpoints or intermediate sums before calculating a grouped estimate, and keep units on the final answer.

## Exam lens

Paper 2 style: know whether the calculator reports population or sample standard deviation and use the symbol requested. This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

Do not describe an estimated grouped mean as exact when the class members' individual values were not supplied.

## Try it yourself

Find the mean and median of $1,2,9$.

## Checked answer

Mean $4$; median $2$. The large value pulls the mean upward.
