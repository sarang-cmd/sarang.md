# Data reliability and sampling

> **SL 4.1 · AA SL foundation (also studied at HL)** · Original Sarang.md course lesson, not an IB publication.

## Understand the idea

Statistical conclusions depend on how data were collected. A large sample does not automatically fix selection bias. A representative design should give relevant population members a known opportunity to be included and should avoid wording that steers answers.

## Formula, meaning and conditions

A simple random sample selects units so every eligible unit has an equal chance under the design. A stratified sample divides the population into meaningful groups and samples within each group. Reliability, validity and representativeness describe different properties of a study.

## Worked example

A school has $600$ lower-year and $400$ upper-year students. For a proportional stratified sample of $100$, choose $100(600/1000)=60$ lower-year and $100(400/1000)=40$ upper-year students randomly within the two strata. Surveying only the first 100 students to enter the library would be convenient but could overrepresent frequent library users.

## Why the method works

A large sample reduces random variation but cannot cure a biased selection method. Random selection means every member has a known chance under the intended design; a social-media poll usually misses people who never see it. Stratification deliberately represents meaningful groups, whereas convenience sampling chooses what is easy to reach. Reliability of a measure and validity of its interpretation are separate questions.

## A contrasting worked route

If a school has $600$ first-year and $400$ second-year students, a proportional stratified sample of $100$ uses $60$ and $40$ respectively. Within each group select at random. Taking the first $60$ and $40$ to arrive is not random within strata and may overrepresent early commuters.

## Transfer and validation

Before calculating a summary statistic, ask who could appear in the data and who could not. A repeated measurement may be consistent yet systematically miscalibrated. Describe a plausible bias without claiming its exact size.

## Exam lens

Paper 2 style: state the population, explain the sampling mechanism, and name a specific possible bias rather than only saying “biased.” This is a practice lens, not a prediction of an examination question.

## A mistake worth catching

A random draw from an incomplete list is still vulnerable to coverage bias. Ask who never appeared on the list.

## Try it yourself

A population is 30% group A. In a proportional sample of 80, how many should come from A?

## Checked answer

$0.30(80)=24$, selected randomly within A.
